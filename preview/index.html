    County guide

    Westchester didn’t say yes everywhere

    Westchester is not one market but dozens. Under the state’s legalisation law, each city, town and village could refuse to host retail dispensaries — and roughly twenty of them did, before the deadline of 31 December 2021 closed the option for good. That is why whole stretches of the county have no shops at all. A town and the village inside it decided separately, which is why some names appear twice.

      Method

      How to read this register

      Where the data comes from

      The spine is the state’s own licence registry, published as the Current OCM Licenses dataset. Phone numbers, hours and menus come from operators’ own sites — each recorded with its source. Aggregators are never used to establish that a shop is licensed; they list unlicensed businesses too.

      What the labels mean

      Registry-verified — licence number and address both matched in the state registry. Secondary sources — two independent sources agree, but no registry match yet; provisional. Unverified — anything less, kept out of the default view.

      Unknown is not the same as no

      A field reading Unknown means nobody checked it, not that the answer is no. The same goes for opening status: an active licence does not mean open doors — New York issued many licences long before the shops were built.

      This directory lists businesses licensed by the New York State Office of Cannabis Management. It is informational only: it does not sell cannabis, and it is not affiliated with or endorsed by any state agency. Cannabis is for adults 21 and over.

      Always confirm a shop yourself before you buy — look for the QR-coded verification decal at the entrance, or search the address at cannabis.ny.gov/dispensary-location-verification.
